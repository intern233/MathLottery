## 1. Involutions: a public corrected problem with no gap found in the written proof

The newly retrieved [arXiv v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14367v2) states the corrected four patterns on p.17. Comparing each diagram confirmed the sample's underlying permutations, shaded coordinates R and R′, and involution scope. The authors had acknowledged that the v1 four-pattern statement was false; the sample does not use that version.

The already solved version for all permutations must be distinguished. [Zhang–Zhao's July paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04111) gives a marked-occurrence bijection establishing distribution equivalence on all permutations. Its five-page full text was read in this audit and did not supply the involution restriction or edgewise inversion compatibility required here. It is a methodological precedent that must remain credited; its completed classification is not a new result of this project.

The [layered-permutation paper](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/97484/) was published online on 2026-10-15. Its full text was obtained, and its definitions and results were confirmed to concern layered permutations; they do not imply the result for all involutions. The official abstract of the [alternating-permutation preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7425138) was accessible, but the PDF request returned 403. Unread content was not ruled out. Together with the latest revision retaining the conjecture, this supports “potentially new,” not certification of worldwide priority.

Substantive checks of the [written proof](mesh-involutions/proof.md) included:

- Equivalence between shaded empty rectangles and the three L/M/Q conditions, with strict boundaries handled by permutation injectivity.
- Star and path components for arbitrary marked subsets, with noncrossing forcing components to nest within a single gap.
- Necessary and sufficient external conditions for both component arrangements; checking outer rotations' effects on inner components covered simultaneous rotation.
- Preservation of component vertex sets in both constructions, two-sided inverse identities, and transposition compatibility for objects and every marked edge.
- Separate weights for orbits of sizes 1 and 2 in transposition-invariant marked sets on involutions. Substituting u=y−1 and v=y²−1 recovers the coefficient for every occurrence count. Weighting only by the total number of marked edges would be insufficient; the proof uses the required two-variable method.
- Reverse complement preserving involutions and extending the result to the other two patterns, including the empty permutation and unmarked cases.

A newly written checker defined occurrences directly from shaded cells without calling the old checker. It enumerated 46,234 permutations with n≤8 and 130,354 marked objects on each of the A and B sides. Graph shapes, validity after rotation, two-sided inverses, object and edgewise transposition compatibility, fixed-edge weights, and four-pattern involution histograms all passed. These finite checks search for errors; the written proof carries the general claim.

**The Lean gap remains.** The final theorem still assumes that `MarkedTransport n` exists for every n. The global marked bijection has not been constructed in Lean. The 107 partial results and historical replay do not replace a formal certificate for the full conjecture. This audit did not upgrade the case to full Lean verification.

## 2. Medial triangle: matching problem statement and successful proof and Lean checks

The audit retrieved the 11-page PDF again from the [official journal](https://ijopcm.zuj.edu.jo/index.php/IJOPCM/article/download/56/43) and inspected p.51. The weights are indeed max{x,y} and max{y,z}, the exponent is any positive integer, the point lies inside the triangle formed by the side midpoints, and the inequality is strict. The problem matches the sample and main Lean theorem; the sample also covers the boundary.

All three steps of the written proof were checked again. Apollonius' identity and five differences of squares yield actual geometric distance bounds. Convexity extends the strict total-distance bound from the vertices to the whole medial triangle. The power-sum lemma and both possible orderings of the maximum weights establish the result for all positive weights and n≥1. Checks covered the positivity needed for square comparisons, n=1, equilateral triangles, zero barycentric coefficients, and equality of the two maximum weights. No omission was found.

The three showcase sources were copied byte for byte into a new build directory using the pinned official Lean/mathlib dependencies. Old candidate build modules were not reused. Successful checks were:

| --- | --- |
| Compile MedialAlgebra, MedialGeometry, and Conjecture5 from source | Exit code 0 for all three |
| Replay those three modules with leanchecker | Exit code 0 |
| Full type and transitive axioms of conjecture5_original | Matches the problem; only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound |
| Proof source hashes against the showcase | Match |

The first two attempts stopped because of the address-space limit and the 2 GiB Lean allocation limit imposed for this audit; the failed records were retained. A later single-process continuation in the same new build directory succeeded without proof changes. This audit replayed the three named modules using official dependencies; it did not repeat `--fresh` replay of every imported dependency. Historical full-dependency replay and this audit's checks are recorded separately.

The formal statement uses the actual Euclidean plane, an affinely independent triangle, the convex hull of actual midpoints, all positive weights, and positive integer exponents. It does not assume the distance bounds being proved. No extra contradictory assumptions, proof placeholders, or custom axioms were found. Correspondence between the written proof and formal statement is an AI assessment from this audit, not external expert certification.

Novelty remains uncertain. The [official 2025 acceptance list](https://shuxuetongxun.com/journal/teacher/2025/4) confirms Hongliang Fei's local-strengthening article, and the original paper cites its first-power result, but the full text was not obtained. The abstract of a [related paper dated 2026-09-15](https://journals.uol.edu.pk/JNHM/article/view/5387) discusses higher powers and multiple-point extensions of another class of moving-point triangle inequalities; its official PDF returned 403. A title or abstract alone neither establishes that it solves this problem nor rules out overlap. There is insufficient evidence to replace UNKNOWN with confirmed priority for solving a currently open problem.

## 3. Stepwise irregular graphs: a complete counterexample with uncertain priority

The audit retrieved the [original paper](https://www.ejgta.org/index.php/ejgta/article/download/1077/pdf_357) again and checked the simple-connected-graph convention and SI definition on p.216, plus the full conjecture on p.223. The sample sequence (6⁴,5⁶,4¹,3¹,2³,1¹) satisfies the three conditions: nonempty degree layers, n₅≥max{6,n₆}, and equal odd- and even-degree sums.

An 8×8 binary matrix was independently transcribed from the proof's adjacency list and checked with a newly written standard-library program, without calling the bundled checker. It yielded a simple connected bipartite graph with 16 vertices and 34 edges, exactly the claimed degree multiplicities, and odd- and even-degree sums of 34 each. The transcribed matrix also matched the attached JSON certificate.

Non-realizability follows from a universal counting argument. In an SI graph, the three degree-2 vertices can only connect to vertices of degree 1 or 3. They require 6 incident edges, while those neighboring classes have total degree only 1+3=4. This excludes every SI realization, regardless of search size and without a connectivity assumption. A single instance satisfying all premises is enough to refute the original universal conjecture.

The same necessary inequality already appears explicitly in the [2022 AIMS Mathematics paper](https://www.aimspress.com/aimspress-data/math/2022/5/PDF/math-07-05-485.pdf), p.8687, equation (2.4). Its full text was obtained and checked in this audit. The capacity method therefore cannot be claimed as original. The accessed material contained no identified complete prior refutation of this particular conjecture, but some recent related journal full texts were unavailable. Priority remains UNKNOWN.
